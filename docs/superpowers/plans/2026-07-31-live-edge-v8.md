# Live Edge & PnL Repair — Implementation Plan (v8)

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking. Execute in order V1→V45 (V43–V45 were added mid-execution when concrete work surfaced them — see Progress block).

**Goal:** Make the live paper book profitable by (a) guaranteeing every winning trade banks **≥2.5%**, (b) cutting the two cohorts that produce ~100% of the losses, and (c) retraining the whole parameter surface on the **25 years of daily history already on disk** instead of the bull-heavy 2020–2023 window that produced the current settings. Then close every live-verification task that plans v2–v7 deferred for lack of a live server.

**Architecture:** A new `MIN_TARGET_PCT` floor is applied inside `plan_engine`'s four sizing helpers so TP1 can never be smaller than a fixed percentage of entry, paired with a *reachability screen* in the scan pipeline that refuses to alert setups whose structure cannot support that move. The gatekeeper (built, tested, and currently switched off) becomes the loss-side filter. Training moves from `data/backtest_cache/` — which does not exist on the live server — onto a no-network bridge from the existing `market_data/daily/` corpus.

**Tech Stack:** Python 3.11, pandas/numpy, pytest, existing `scripts/` backtest + walk-forward harness. No new dependencies. No ML in the live path.

### Root cause is structural

`plan_engine.py:170-197` computes `TP1 = entry ± risk_distance × rr`, with `rr` from
`STRATEGY_RR_OVERRIDE` (`strategy_types.py:217-232`, all values 0.30–0.40) floored at
`RR_FLOOR = 0.30` (`plan_engine.py:31`). The stop is the *full* `risk_distance` (1R).
A win therefore banks ~0.35R while a loss costs ~1R **by construction**:

- median designed TP1 distance = **0.85%** (v2 confluence path: **0.77%**)
- median designed stop distance = **2.19%**
- median designed R:R = **0.35**

The median winning trade *cannot arithmetically* reach 2%. This was deliberate —
tight targets buying a high win rate (`strategy_types.py:210-216` targets 80%+ WR) —
and is recorded as a frozen constant in `docs/claude/backtest-methodology.md:15`.
Live, the win rate bought (55.6%) is far below the win rate required (63.2%).

Journal exit efficiency confirms the waste: median **0.24**. The median winner had
**0.92R** of favourable excursion available; we took **0.35R**.

### Is 90% win rate reachable at 2.5% targets? The evidence says no — read before setting expectations.

The 90% target and the 2.5% floor pull in opposite directions, and the original
system already made this trade *the other way*: it bought a high win rate precisely
by making targets tiny (0.35R), which is exactly what produced the +1.44% average win
this plan exists to fix. Win rate at a given target is not a free parameter.

Measured from the live journal (267 usable MFE entries), win rate by cohort as the
target widens — the median stop is 2.19%, so **2.5% ≈ 1.14R**:

| cohort | n | @0.35R (today, ~0.85%) | @0.9R (~2.0%) | @1.25R (~2.7%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| tier A | 30 | **90%** | 70% | 53% |
| tier B | 82 | **95%** | 59% | 46% |
| tier C | 142 | 84% | 35% | 28% |

**Every cohort hits ~90% only at the tiny targets we are removing.** At 2.5% the best
cohort (tier A) sits near **65%**, and that estimate is *optimistic* — the same method
predicts +0.13R for tier C, which actually lost money live, so it runs ~0.3R hot.

This does not make the directive wrong; it makes it a **search target rather than a
promise**. The legitimate path to a high win rate at 2.5% is a far more selective
entry filter than "tier A" — the top decile of a retuned quality score, plus regime
alignment, plus gatekeeper A+ — accepting very low trade counts. V17 and V20 search
for exactly that frontier. What the plan must not do is claim 90% by shrinking the
target back down, which would rebuild the original problem.

**Expected honest outcome: a small, highly selective cohort somewhere in the 65–80%
band at ≥2.5% wins, with positive expectancy.** That is a far better book than
today's 55.6% at +1.44%/−2.47%. If the search does find a ≥90% cohort at N ≥ 59,
excellent — but the plan is not built on assuming it exists.

### Task V43: Document the cache-overwrite data-loss trap — **done 2026-08-02**

**Files:** `docs/claude/known-traps.md` (add alongside the existing two-cache entry)

Discovered 2026-07-31 while extending gold/silver history. **`download_and_cache()`
→ `save_to_disk()` → `df.to_csv(path)` is a destructive overwrite with no merge.**
Only `update_cache()` merges (incremental, appends bars newer than the CSV's last
date, atomic replace).

This is not theoretical. Yahoo serves 1h bars for a **rolling ~730-day window**, but
the on-disk hourly cache has accumulated *past* that window by running incrementally
for months. Measured the same day:

| | Yahoo will serve today | On disk | Delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| `GC=F` hourly | 11,488 bars from 2024-07-31 | **13,758** from 2024-03-08 | +2,270 |
| `SI=F` hourly | 11,486 bars from 2024-07-31 | **13,854** from 2024-03-03 | +2,368 |
| `NVDA` hourly | ~730 days | 5,110 bars from **2023-08-25** | ~1 extra year |

So calling `download_and_cache(t, "hourly")` across the watchlist would silently
delete roughly **a year of intraday history for all 83 tickers**, and Yahoo will
never serve it again — the loss is permanent. Daily is unaffected (full history is
always re-servable; both metals cap at 2000-08-30, confirmed via `period=max` and
`start=1970`).

- [x] **Step 1 (done 2026-07-31, alongside V1):** The trap is in
      `docs/claude/known-traps.md` — which function overwrites, which merges, why
      hourly is irreplaceable, the measured `GC=F` numbers, and the rule. Extended
      2026-08-02 to record that the rule is now *enforced* rather than remembered.
- [x] **Step 2 (done 2026-07-31, in V1 itself):** `seed_backtest_cache.py` refuses to

- [x] **Step 3 (done 2026-08-02):** `save_to_disk(..., allow_shrink=False)` raises

      measured and are not re-scored. **Everything from V51 onward uses this

      *Revised the same day, before anything executed under it, by a second
      directive: the bar is **80%** (not 85%) and the loss cap is **1.75%** (not
      2.0%). The figures below are the revised ones throughout.*

      OBJECTIVE   maximise expectancy_r
      SUBJECT TO  win_rate >= 80%          <- hard constraint, was a 90% stretch
                  every win >= 2.5%         (unchanged)
                  every loss <= 1.75%       <- NEW hard cap on realised loss
                  cut sooner than 1.75% when the entry thesis is already invalid
                  scratches + timeouts <= 50% of closed trades
      NO CEILING  no maximum on profit -- winners run on the trail, no fixed tp2
      FLOOR       reject any config with win_rate < 80% regardless of expectancy

      **Reached in three pre-registered stages — 60%, then 70%, then 80%** (second
      directive, 2026-08-02). Each stage is a gate, not a milestone: a stage that
      cannot be cleared at provable N stops the ladder and the achieved frontier is
      the result. See V52 for the stage definitions and their exit criteria.

      Two changes of kind, not just of number. **Win rate moves from objective to
      constraint**, so it can no longer be bought by shrinking targets — the search
      maximises expectancy *among* configs that already clear the bar. And **the
      loss side is now bounded**, which the old rule never constrained at all.

      **Direction-of-effect warning, recorded before the search runs.** The 1.75%
      loss cap is *well tighter than the current median stop of 2.19%* (measured,
      see "Is 90% reachable?" above), and cutting sooner converts trades that would
      have recovered into realised losses. **Both new constraints push win rate
      down while the bar moves up.** Tightening 2.00% -> 1.75% was measured to cost
      **3.2 points of win rate** (46.6% -> 43.4% at no skill, V52 groundwork), so
      the revision moved the bar down 5 points and the floor down 3.2 — the net
      gain in reachability is about 1.8 points, not 5. The only lever that raises
      win rate at a fixed 2.5% target is entry selectivity. V52 searches
      selectivity; it does not search targets.

      **The bar is about consistency, not profitability.** At 2.5% win / 1.75% loss
      the payoff is **1.4286R**, so break-even is **41.2%** and the economics are
      already strong far below the bar: 43.4% (no skill) -> +0.054R,
      50% -> +0.214R, 60% -> +0.457R, 70% -> +0.700R, 80% -> +0.943R. A config at
      70% that clears every other constraint is a *far better book than today* and
      must be reported as such even though the rule rejects it.

      **Sample size per stage** (Wilson LB > bar, z=1.96, `wr_math.py`, the
      function V6 Step 5 used). Read this as the cost of each rung:

      | observed WR | N to prove 60% | N to prove 70% | N to prove 80% |
      |---|---|---|---|
      | 100% | 9 | 13 | 21 |
      | 95% | 9 | 18 | 29 |
      | 90% | 13 | 23 | 64 |
      | 85% | 16 | 41 | 256 |
      | 80% | 26 | 87 | — |
      | 75% | 42 | 330 | — |

      This is the trap in the directive and it must not be glossed. Selectivity
      buys win rate by cutting N, but **the closer the measured rate sits to the
      bar, the more N is needed to prove it** — clearing 80% on an 85%-observed
      cohort needs 256 independent trades, which a heavily selective screen will
      not produce. A cohort is only ADOPTABLE where the Wilson lower bound clears
      the bar, never the point estimate. Report both, always, and treat
      point-estimate-only cohorts as hypotheses exactly as V6 Step 5 requires.

      **Swing, not day trade (second directive, 2026-08-02).** Recorded because the
      measurement contradicts the intuition: with fixed ±2.5%/1.75% barriers the
      no-skill outcome is **flat from 1m to 9m** (43.4% at every rung) and
      **99.3% of entries resolve inside one month** — timeouts fall to 0.7% by 1m
      and 0.0% by 6m. Barrier distance, not the horizon label, sets the holding
      period, which is why the live book shows a ~1.2h median winning hold under
      0.35R targets. **TP1 at 2.5% will therefore resolve fast even on a 9m
      horizon; the swing character has to come from the uncapped runner** (V51
      Step 2), not from TP1. Do not read a short median hold as the system
      day-trading — read it as TP1 being near. Any claim that a config is
      "swing-like" must be evidenced by *runner* holding periods, not TP1's.
