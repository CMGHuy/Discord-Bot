# v86 — The cohort risk label: telling a reader when trades like this one have lost money

**Bump:** bot minor, ui patch
**Edge:** none (integrity) — see §7
**Date:** 2026-09-14

## 1. Why this exists

The live paper book measured on 2026-09-10 is net negative: N=782 closed,
WR 53.5%, **ExpR −0.136R**, profit factor 0.727. `source` shows 624/782 = 80%
of it is confluence-sourced, and the confluence scan is the only negative
population in the book — it mirrors its own backtest pool (53.5% / −0.171R,
N=4641) closely enough that the live book is largely that one known-negative
population playing out.

The existing levers on that population are already tuned or already closed:
`MIN_TARGET_CONFLUENCE_COUNT` and `min_confidence` are grid-derived, the RS
gate ships bullish-half-disabled (`RS_LEADER_PERCENTILE=0`), and `REGIME_ALLOW`
ships **empty by evidence** after the v17 P2a fold. There is no dial left to
turn.

Two closed results constrain what may be attempted:

- **No third blended-confidence-score.** v32 (unified confidence) and v33 (MTF
  adjacent gate) both regressed on TRAIN and shipped disabled. Two independent
  failures of "combine signals into one score" points at the feature set, not
  the weights.
- **`REGIME_ALLOW` is a closed pre-registration** — a binary hard gate applied
  pool-wide. Anything regime-flavoured must differ in mechanism *and* scope.

This spec re-runs neither. It adds a **lookup**, not a score: a frozen table of
how trades under given conditions have actually closed, surfaced to the reader
as a warning on the plans that fall in the bad cells. Nothing is suppressed.
The decision the label exists to inform is taken later, from measured
separation, under a pre-registration written here (§6).

## 2. Decisions taken in the brainstorm (human partner, 2026-09-14)

1. The target is **fewer, better setups** — the confluence pool specifically.
2. A failing setup is **still issued**, carrying a "be careful, this has a high
   loss rate" label. Real performance gets checked after it has run a while.
3. The cohort is drawn from **both** the backtest pool and the live book,
   blended.
4. The v1 key is **`direction × regime2` (8 cells)**, not the 24-cell key that
   adds confidence — see §3.1.

## 3. The cohort registry

A second registry beside the validation one, following the same contract:
`swingbot/core/backtesting/cohort_registry.json`, generated by a script,
**committed and frozen**, carrying `n` / `win_rate` / `expectancy_r` / `window`
/ `run_date` per cell. Where `validation_registry.json` answers *"has this
strategy passed a pre-registration?"*, this answers *"how have trades under
these conditions actually closed?"* — a different question on a different key.

### 3.1 The key, and why it is only two dimensions

`direction` (2) × `regime2` state (4) = **8 cells**.

Cell arithmetic drives this, not interest. Against the live book (N=782) that
is ~98/cell; against the backtest pool (N=4641), ~580/cell. Every cell is fat.
By contrast `strategy × horizon` is 460 cells over 782 trades and is already
noise-dominated — the existing win-rate heatmap is a measured example of the
failure mode.

A third dimension (`confidence_level`, 24 cells) was considered and
**deliberately deferred**. `backtest_scenarios.py` is a real historical replay —
it rebuilds the level map per bar and feeds scenarios through the same
`build_confluence_plan` — but it never calls `score_confidence`. That is not an
oversight: `scripts/backtest/v32_validation.py` exists as a separate one-shot
script precisely because the normal backtest path "cannot test
`score_confidence()`/`MIN_ALERT_CONFIDENCE_LEVEL` gating". Adding scoring to the
replay is feasible (v32_validation is the working NO-LOOKAHEAD precedent) but
costs a slower replay and a re-run of a path that has already published numbers.
A second unknown sits behind it: `plan.confidence_level` is stamped by
`_apply_quality`, but whether older live closed trades carry it is unverified.

§4's feature stamping collects `confidence_level` on every new plan regardless.
The right order is to measure whether confidence separates outcomes *inside* a
`direction × regime2` cell, then pay to backfill it into the replay only if it
does.

This is also what finally puts `regime2` in the decision path. It is computed
today at `swingbot/core/scanning/analyze.py:146` and consumed only by chart
shading (`swingbot/core/charts/decision_chart.py:127`).

### 3.2 The blending rule

Backtest is the prior, live is the update, one parameter:

```
p_blend = (n_live · p_live + K · p_backtest) / (n_live + K)        K = 100
```

Beta-binomial shrinkage, applied identically to win rate and to ExpR. A cell
with few live trades reads essentially as the backtest base rate; once live
trades pass `K` the live number dominates. One pseudo-count constant, chosen
and frozen, **not** grid-tuned — a tuned `K` would be a fitted parameter
masquerading as a prior.

Cells below a hard floor (`n_live + n_backtest < 50`) resolve to `UNKNOWN`,
which renders as "not enough data" and never as "safe".

### 3.3 The label is relative, not absolute

The confluence pool as a whole sits at −0.136R. An absolute threshold would fire
on nearly every alert and would train the reader to ignore it. Each cell is
therefore scored against the pool's own blended mean:

| Label | Rule | Renders |
|---|---|---|
| `COHORT_POOR` | blended ExpR materially below pool mean | the caution line |
| `COHORT_TYPICAL` | within the band | nothing |
| `COHORT_STRONG` | materially above pool mean | a positive stat line |
| `COHORT_UNKNOWN` | below the N floor | the thin-data note |

"Materially" is one frozen margin constant: **±0.15R against the pool mean**.
A cell at or below `pool_mean − 0.15R` is `COHORT_POOR`; at or above
`pool_mean + 0.15R`, `COHORT_STRONG`; between them, `COHORT_TYPICAL`. Chosen
once and frozen, not grid-tuned — it sets what the reader is told, and a tuned
margin would be fitted to the very outcomes §6 is meant to test.

The caution text names the cohort and its measured numbers, the way
`badge_stats_line` does — a vague warning teaches nothing:

> ⚠️ **Cohort:** bearish setups in a volatile-bear regime have closed
> **41.2% WR / −0.38R** (n=214, frozen 2026-09-14). Reduced size, manual
> confirmation.

### 3.4 Forward-only by construction

The table is frozen at a `run_date`. Each plan persists `cohort_stats` exactly
as it persists `badge_stats`, so §6's verification runs **only on plans created
strictly after the freeze** and knows precisely what each plan was told at
issuance. Without this the table would be scored against the very trades that
built it, and the whole "check the real performance later" step would be
meaningless.

## 4. Feature stamping

New field on `TradePlanV2`: `risk_features: dict`, stamped at issuance in
`analyze.py` where the bar context lives. **NO-LOOKAHEAD: every feature is
computed from the creating bar and earlier only** (`docs/claude/architecture.md`).
`journal.build_entry` copies it into the journal entry at close, so every closed
trade carries what was knowable about it at birth.

| Feature | Source |
|---|---|
| `regime2_state` | `analyze.py:146`, already computed |
| `confidence_level` | `item.conf.level`, already on the plan |
| `htf_agree` | HTF bias vs. direction; `score_confidence` already takes `htf_bias` |
| `confluence_count` | already computed by the gate |
| `dist_to_level_atr` | entry extension — the "chasing it" hypothesis |
| `stop_width_atr` | plan stop distance in ATR |
| `atr_pct` | name-level volatility, distinct from market regime |
| `rs_percentile` | computed today, discarded on the long side (`RS_LEADER_PERCENTILE=0`) |
| `session_bucket` | issuance time; median live hold is ~2h, so intraday timing plausibly matters more than the 2w–9m labels suggest |
| `days_to_earnings` | **opportunistic** — null unless v82's calendar is merged; never a hard dependency |

Plus `scripts/reports/cohort_separation_report.py`: reads journal + plans and
reports outcome separation per feature. This is what makes *"what should the
label have been keyed on?"* answerable from data rather than guessable.

**Coordination flag:** a new persisted plan field touches v67's json-to-postgres
plans table. Not a blocker; the implementation plan carries a task that mirrors
the field rather than discovering the mismatch at cutover.

## 6. The pre-registration, written before the data exists

Registered now, in this spec, because deciding the cut after seeing the answers
is how a measurement stops meaning anything.

- **Claim:** plans labelled `COHORT_POOR` close at materially worse ExpR than
  plans labelled `COHORT_TYPICAL` or `COHORT_STRONG`.
- **Population:** confluence-source plans created **strictly after** the
  registry's `run_date`, closed.
- **Minimum N before the question may be asked:** **150 closed plans in each
  group** (`COHORT_POOR`, and `TYPICAL`+`STRONG` together) — per group, never
  pooled. `UNKNOWN` plans are excluded from both. Below that, the question is
  not asked and no interim read is quoted.
- **Decision rule:** PASS iff
  `ExpR(POOR) ≤ ExpR(non-POOR) − 0.20R` with both groups at N ≥ 150.
  The separation bar exceeds the ±0.15R banding margin deliberately: a label
  that only reproduces its own construction margin has demonstrated nothing.
  PASS makes a suppression flip a *candidate*; it does not authorise one. FAIL
  retires the key and sends §4's separation report looking for a better one.
- **One shot.** This is a closed pre-registration on the day it is committed. It
  is not re-run at a larger N, on a different window, or with a different
  margin, whatever the first read says.
- **A flip is its own later release** (`bot patch`: fewer alerts, same shape),
  never part of this bump.

## 7. Bump and Edge, argued

**`Bump:` bot minor** — the alert channel gains a new line type; a reader of
yesterday's channel sees messages of a shape they have not seen. **ui patch** —
one chip on the trade detail.

**`Edge:` none (integrity).** Stated plainly: no exit, gate, fill or sizing rule
changes, so pooled paper `ExpR` is unchanged **by construction**. What this
release buys is the human partner's real-account behaviour — they trade real
money off these alerts — and the evidence substrate a suppression decision would
need. The `expectancy` claim belongs to the flip in §6, and only if it passes.
